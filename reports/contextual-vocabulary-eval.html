Vale enumerated all seven prose candidates and skipped the inline-code occurrence. The systematic problem is not enumeration: Jev sometimes fails to recognize that an unhyphenated phrase modifies the following noun.

What the fixtures taught us

The current surface form can bias classification: an already hyphenated phrase sometimes looks like a modifier even when a verb follows it. Literal quotations are also boundary cases. Marking the exact occurrence and asking an observable syntactic question helped, but did not eliminate either issue.

The next experiment should test a deterministic part-of-speech or dependency cue before Jev, while retaining Jev for UI labels, official names, quotations, and genuinely ambiguous syntax.
